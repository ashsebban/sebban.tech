Learn to play

How to play

  • Checkmate. You play Genghis Khan’s Mongol army as White. The computer plays the Chinese army as Black. Checkmate the opposing ruler to win.
  • Mongol horse archers. Move or capture like a knight, or shoot exactly two squares along a clear rank or file and then jump. A landing capture can happen on the same turn.
  • Genghis Khan. A friendly queen grants long slides, a friendly knight grants knight jumps and shoot-and-jump, and a friendly bishop grants the Khan the ability to shoot over friendly pawns. Mongol pawns and bishops use their regular chess moves.
  • Chinese army. The Chinese king and knights use their regular chess moves. Pawns on Black’s second rank are walls. Friendly pawns through Black’s fourth rank are walls while a horizontal or diagonal chain connects them to that second rank. Cut the connection and the isolated pawns become infantry.
  • Chinese bishops. A friendly pawn immediately ahead or beside the bishop lets it shoot straight over that pawn. With a fortified wall as cover, it can also shoot one lane left or right where a friendly pawn supports that lane. It can step forward or sideways behind a friendly wall, or slide behind an unbroken pawn line. Arrows cannot break walls.
  • Castles. Castles move like rooks. Either army can spend a castle’s turn returning a captured friendly pawn to an empty adjacent square in its own half (White’s ranks 1–4 or Black’s ranks 5–8), up to eight pawns. Standard castling is available to both rulers.
  • Draws. Threefold repetition, stalemate, or 60 turns without a pawn move, reinforcement, or capture is a draw.
