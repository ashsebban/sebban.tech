Learn to play

How to play

  • Checkmate. Checkmate the enemy Khan to win. Castles move and capture like rooks.
  • The Khan. The Khan can always step one square like a king. A friendly queen on the board gives him long slides, at least one friendly horse archer gives him knight jumps and shoot-and-jump, and at least one friendly bishop lets him shoot over walls. A pawn that promotes to queen restores the queen power.
  • Castling. Move the Khan two squares toward an original corner castle. Both pieces must be unmoved, with empty squares between them. The Khan cannot castle out of, through, or into check. If a normal Khan move also reaches that square, choose Castling from the move menu.
  • Pawn reinforcements. A castle can spend its turn returning a captured friendly pawn to an empty adjacent square off that pawn’s promotion rank. Its gate lights up when it can return a pawn. Each side can have up to eight pawns on the board.
  • Pawns and walls. Pawns advance straight and capture diagonally. They keep the initial straight double step. On the far rank, choose a queen, castle, bishop, or horse archer. Home-rank pawns on White’s ranks 1–3 or Black’s ranks 6–8 become walls. Enemy pawns, castles, and the Khan can capture walls by moving onto them. Arrows cannot break walls.
  • Bishops. Slide horizontally along an unbroken line of friendly pawns directly ahead, stopping at a gap or a piece. A bishop may capture an enemy at the end of its slide. A friendly wall or infantry pawn immediately ahead, left, or right equips the bishop with a bow. Its arrow reaches only the next square straight beyond that pawn. Wall and phalanx shots can give check.
  • Horse archers. Move or capture like a knight, or shoot an enemy exactly two squares away in a clear row or column and then make a knight jump. The archer can capture an enemy on its landing square as part of the same turn.
  • Draws. Threefold repetition, stalemate, or 60 turns without a pawn move, reinforcement, or capture is a draw.
