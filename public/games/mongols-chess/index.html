How to play

  • Breach a castle. Land a piece on either enemy castle to win. Castles stay put and are immune to arrows.
  • Shoot, then jump. A horse archer can optionally shoot one enemy piece exactly two squares away in a row or column, with an empty square in between. It must then make a knight jump to an empty square. It can also move and capture like a regular knight without shooting.
  • Fire from a castle. A castle can shoot one enemy unit one or two squares away along a clear row or column. Firing takes your turn. Any intervening piece blocks the shot.
  • Call reinforcements. Spend a turn returning a captured friendly pawn to any empty square touching your castle. A returned pawn on its usual starting rank can advance two clear squares on its first move.
  • Walls and battlefield. White pawns on ranks 1–3 and Black pawns on ranks 6–8 are walls. They block arrows and resist capture. Ranks 4 and 5 are the battlefield, where pawns can be captured normally.
  • Bishop sidestep. With a friendly wall one square straight or diagonally ahead, a bishop can step one square left or right into an empty square.
  • Other pieces. Pawns, bishops, queens, and kings use their normal movement. Pawns promote to queens. Kings can be captured, and play continues until a castle falls or the game is drawn.
  • Draws. Threefold repetition, neither side able to move, or 60 turns without a pawn move, reinforcement, or capture is a draw. A side with no legal move passes.
